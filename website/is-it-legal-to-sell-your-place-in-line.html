Guide · Canada

Is it legal to sell your place in line in Canada?

Short answer: we know of no Canadian law that bans handing your place in a line to someone else. The details depend on the place, the line and what is being exchanged.

What the law says

We are not aware of any federal or provincial law in Canada that prohibits letting another person take over your place in a line. In 2022, a federal service agency told journalists that its rules about applying on someone else's behalf do not apply to people who are simply waiting in line.

That does not make every line a free-for-all. The place, the line and what is being exchanged all matter.

Where rules can still apply

  • Venue and business rules. Many venues, stores and attractions set their own terms for lines. A rule at the door can be enforced even where no law applies.
  • Tickets are different. Some provinces regulate ticket resale, including price limits. A place in line is not a ticket, but if you also hand over a ticket, the ticket rules apply to it.
  • Government services. An office may still require the applicant to be there in person when their turn comes. A place in line is not an appointment.
  • Public space. Keep sidewalks and entrances clear, follow local bylaws, and listen to staff or police if they ask the line to move.
  • Income. Money you earn from selling places in line may be taxable income. Keep your records.

How QSpot Marketplace fits in

Every trade on QSpot™ Marketplace is a one-for-one swap. The seller is already in the line on their own initiative, steps out as the buyer steps in, and nobody behind them moves back. Nobody is hired to wait for anyone.

With secure payment, the buyer's card is held and only charged once both people are at the line and the handover is confirmed by GPS.

Before you list your spot

  • Check whether the place has its own rules about lines.
  • If a ticket or booking is involved, check the resale rules for your province.
  • Be at the line, and check in at least once an hour while your spot is listed.

This page is general information, not legal advice. Laws differ by province and can change. For your own situation, ask a lawyer.

Questions

Can I sell my place in line in Quebec?

We are not aware of a Quebec rule that is specific to places in line. Quebec has its own consumer and ticket-resale rules, so check them if a ticket is part of the trade.

Is it legal to pay someone to wait in line for me?

We are not aware of a law against it either, but venue rules and in-person requirements still apply. QSpot works differently: nobody is hired, and a person already in the line hands over their own place.
